PRJ-001

Smart Pillow — Sleep-Monitoring System

Working prototype

A non-invasive pillow that observes sleep through embedded sensors — no wearables, no straps — and estimates core body temperature with a fuzzy-logic model.

Three temperature sensors and a humidity sensor are distributed inside the pillow body. A temperature gradient across the sensors reveals head position; humidity trends indicate night sweating. Data streams over BLE to a gateway and on to a cloud dashboard with trends and alerts for families, schools and elderly-care programs.

PRJ-002

Air Map — Real-Time Air-Quality Ecosystem

1I1W 2026 — Gold Medal

An IoT network that makes Hanoi's invisible air pollution tangible — a physical LED map of the city glows green, yellow or red district by district, in real time.

Custom ESP32 sensing nodes measure PM2.5/PM10 dust plus temperature and humidity, and push readings over Wi-Fi to the ThingSpeak cloud. A central controller pulls the data back via REST API to drive addressable LEDs embedded in a laser-cut 3D map, while a companion mobile app (MIT App Inventor) shows AQI charts and history. Awarded a Gold Medal at 1 Idea 1 World (1I1W) 2026, an international innovation, design and startup competition held in Istanbul, Türkiye (March 2026).

PRJ-003

AI & IoT for Traffic Congestion in Hanoi — Research Paper

Published — IJMSM 2026

A co-authored research paper proposing an AI- and IoT-based traffic-management framework for Hanoi, where fixed-time traffic signals cannot keep up with fast-changing, motorcycle-heavy traffic.

The paper reviews two decades of adaptive traffic-signal research — from Q-learning and fuzzy / ANFIS controllers tested on Hanoi intersections to multi-agent deep reinforcement learning — and proposes a four-layer architecture in which IoT sensors feed AI models that predict congestion and adjust signal timing in a closed loop. It also analyses Hanoi's real deployment: in December 2025 the city connected 1,837 cameras with 188 AI-enabled signal-control cabinets and set up green-wave corridors. Written with researchers from HUST and students from HUS High School for Gifted Students, Hanoi–Amsterdam and Le Quy Don (Da Nang).

PRJ-004

Classroom Helper — AI Teaching Assistant Prototype

Fulbright Vietnam 2026

A voice-controlled classroom assistant prototype developed during the Fulbright Vietnam 2026 STEM Mentorship Program. It pairs a Windows desktop overlay with a browser-based interface for quick use in lessons and demos.

Users can ask questions in Vietnamese, open files by name, check their timetable, recap previous lessons, control presentation slides, and record transcripts with AI-generated lesson summaries. The prototype can also import a timetable from a photo and save lesson notes.
